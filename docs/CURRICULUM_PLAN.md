# Curriculum Plan

This curriculum is mastery-based and adaptive. Learners move by demonstrated understanding, not by seat time. The system uses mentor agents to teach across domains with consistent goals, strategies, and artifacts.

## Principles

- Mastery over pacing: learners advance when ready, reinforce when needed.
- Evidence over answers: reasoning is required for transfer and depth.
- Multiple pathways: content is reframed to match interests and strengths.
- Creation over compliance: artifacts and projects prove understanding.
- Safety and dignity: no shaming, no gotchas, no punitive scoring.

## Learning progression

- Discovery: curiosity-led exploration with short, low-stakes prompts.
- Fluency: accurate, fast recall of core knowledge and skills.
- Reasoning: explain why, connect concepts, spot contradictions.
- Application: solve multi-step, real-world problems.
- Creation: build original work and reflect on choices.
- Mentorship: teach others and lead collaborative work.

## Domains and focus areas

- Humanities
  - History and civics: systems, incentives, rights, tradeoffs.
  - Literature and storytelling: narrative, rhetoric, voice, revision.
  - Media literacy: sources, bias, evidence, argument quality.
- Mathematics
  - Numeracy and algebra: patterns, functions, proportional reasoning.
  - Geometry: space, measurement, modeling, proof.
  - Data and probability: uncertainty, risk, decision making.
- Science
  - Physical systems: energy, motion, forces.
  - Life systems: ecosystems, heredity, adaptation.
  - Earth and space: cycles, scale, observation.
- Creator skills
  - Design and engineering: constraints, iteration, prototyping.
  - Coding and computation: algorithms, debugging, models.
  - Visual arts and music: composition, critique, expression.
- Life skills
  - Financial literacy: budgeting, tradeoffs, entrepreneurship.
  - Health and wellbeing: habits, decision making, reflection.
  - Communication: speaking, listening, collaboration.

## Assessment signals

- Mastery checks: short adaptive prompts and mini tasks.
- Reasoning transcripts: Socratic dialogue logs.
- Artifacts: essays, proofs, models, prototypes, presentations.
- Portfolios: longitudinal evidence of growth.
- Narrative feedback: clear next steps, not just scores.

## Project studios

- City Builder: budget, civics, geometry, narrative pitch.
- History Court: argue historical decisions with evidence.
- Myths and Machines: write a myth, build a simulation.
- Design a Game: probability, balance, player psychology.
- Science Lab: investigate and publish findings.

## Personalization engine

- Learner profile: strengths, gaps, interests, motivation patterns.
- Pacing bands: accelerate or reinforce dynamically.
- Content variants: multiple contexts for the same skill.
- Attention supports: chunking, coaching, adaptive breaks.

## Collaboration model

- Shared learner profile: all mentors read the same strengths, gaps, and interests.
- Clean handoffs: one mentor ends with a clear next step and intent for the next.
- Co-teaching moments: mentors can tag in to deepen a concept (e.g., Pythagoras for modeling in a Curie lab).
- Consistent evidence standard: reasoning and artifacts stay central across subjects.
- Disagreement protocol: if a student pushes back, mentors ask for evidence and compare tradeoffs.
- Portfolio continuity: artifacts connect across domains to show growth over time.
